### 15 June — "Self-adjoint Picard iteration update mechanism"
David pasted the 8-step algorithm agreed in the (out-of-scope) 14 June codebase thread and asked what the update step actually is.

Settled content:
- **Outer Newton step on `λ = P₁(N_total)`**: residual `r(λ) = φ₁(N_total; λ) − φ_final`; sensitivity `∂φ₁(N_total)/∂λ` via one *linear* backward pass with `δP₁(N_total)=1, δP₂=0` **plus** one linear forward pass `d(δφ)/dN = (∂f/∂P)·δP` from `δφ(0)=0`. David caught that Claude's "linear variation of the backward pass" phrase hid the second pass: "Don't steps 2 and 3 require an extra backwards pass **and** an extra forwards pass?" — conceded.
- **Inner Picard loop**: fixed-point `φ = F(φ)`; backward pass evaluates nonlinear coefficients on `φ_old`, forward pass sourced by `P_new`; convergence norm is over the **whole trajectory**, not the endpoint ("It would be a mistake to conflate it with the inner convergence norm").
- **Complexity**: Picard ≈ `k_outer × (2k_inner + 2)` integrations vs shooting ≈ `(2N+1) k_shoot`; Claude first said shooting wins on count and Picard only on conditioning (adjoint has forward-growing modes). David corrected: shooting is a **2N-dimensional** root-find (unknown `P₁(0), P₂(0)`), Picard a **1D** root-find on `λ` (more generally `N_constrained ≤ N` Lagrange multipliers vs `2N`), with the dense `2N×2N` shooting Jacobian ill-conditioned over long `N_total`. Final tally: `Picard ≈ 2k_outer k_inner + 2N_constrained k_outer`.

**Recorded:** the conditioning argument (`NUMERICAL_SCHEMES.md` §2.2, "φ is stable forward, P is stable backward"). **Not recorded:** the complexity count, the dimension-reduction argument (2N vs N_constrained), the whole-trajectory-norm point, and the fact that the implemented Newton step used a *finite-difference* probe (`02a-implement-jacobian.md`) rather than the sensitivity passes discussed here — later replaced by the secant `ShootingSolver` (§2.4) for exactly the conditioning reason this thread anticipated.

**(a) The `ρ_final` question — resolved, resolution unrecorded.**
- Claude first argued the failure is *geometric, not counting*: one free `λ` can impose one scalar terminal condition; the `λ`-family of endpoints `(φ₁(T;λ), φ₂(T;λ))` is a curve in the `(φ₁,φ₂)` plane that always crosses the hyperplane `φ₁ = const` (monotone in `λ`) but need not meet the `ρ = const` level set; for large `δN★` the endpoint is far off-attractor and they miss. Recommended Option 3: keep `φ_final`, compute `δN★_actual = N_end_downflow − N_end_noiseless` post hoc; invert over `φ_final` if a fixed-`ρ` scan is wanted.
- David then reframed (11:47): the terminal BC is "a bit of a red herring". The Hubble volume's history is two phases, noise-activated dislocation (instanton) then noiseless downflow, "chopped up for computational simplicity". **Dislocation can be permanent** (multi-field relocation, false vacua). "In `FullInstanton` the final boundary condition is just a label that divides the trajectory into these two regions" — it marks where the noise switches off, which sets (i) the scales the perturbation accumulates on, (ii) its sharpness — "Cerro Torre" vs "Olympus Mons" — hence the compaction function. The instanton equations can't find the downflow themselves because "the noiseless evolution isn't rare, so the saddle point approximation … don't apply."

**(b) The Vennin comparison — the stalled argument, in detail.**
- David set up "Vincent's formalism" (Ezquiaga–García-Bellido–Vennin 1912.05399): eigenvalues of the backward Kolmogorov operator on `[N_end−N_init, N_end]`, tail `∼ exp(−λ₀ N★)`. It gives only a tail probability via the spectrum, "not the most probable transition path", so no spatial profile. The apples-to-apples instanton is `N_final = 0` (endpoint exactly at end of inflation), tail `= exp(−S_instanton)`, **no downflow phase**.
- David's central worry (12:40): the Kolmogorov average "really is *all* noise realizations" — noise on, off, on again — so "**we don't know a priori what the eigenvalue tail probability is an estimate for**, because we don't know *where it is putting the noise*." Claude agreed: the eigenvalue method cannot know the collapse criterion; if the cheapest channel is a sub-critical profile, `exp(−λ₀N★)` overestimates PBH abundance; a benchmark is clean only if the dominant eigenfunction's noise support is identified.
- David's conjecture (13:06): expand the instanton in Kolmogorov eigenfunctions, substitute into the MSR action, use orthogonality to get `S_instanton = Σ_i λ_i c_i²`, `c_i = |(instanton, ψ_i)|²`; Kamenev §4.4 (optimal path) / §4.5 (FP, supersymmetry) / §4.9 (Donsker–Varadhan, Green's function above (4.59)) is the scaffold. Claude's first pass asserted the diagonal form and attached it to profile shape (narrow profile → high modes → more suppressed than Vennin).
- **David demanded the explicit substitution** (13:16). Claude did it in slow roll: `H = PA + P²D`, `H=0 ⇒ P = −A/D`, `φ̇₁ = −A` (time-reversed drift), `S = ∫ A²/D dN`; linearise `P ≈ −δφ·∂_φ[A/D]|_bg` (because `P_bg = 0`); `S ≈ ∫ δφ² (A′D − AD′)²/D³ dN`; insert `δφ = Σ c_i ψ_i(φ_bg(N))`; the cross integral `∫ψ_iψ_j w(N) dN = ∫ψ_iψ_j (w/A) dφ` **is not the Sturm–Liouville orthogonality integral** unless `w/A` equals the SL weight, "which would require a specific relationship between `A` and `D` that doesn't hold in general." **Claude: "the clean diagonal form does not follow."** David: "This is where I got stuck. I am pleased you have got stuck here also. Something like this has *got* to work."

David's reframing (13:21): Vennin's calculation is automatically a **sum over instantons**, i.e. integration over the `c_i`. ### 29 June — "Fokker–Planck equation with exponential noise"
- One-sided exponential noise: all cumulants `κ_n = (n−1)!/λⁿ` nonzero, Kramers–Moyal series resums to the pseudo-differential FPE `∂_t P = −∂_x[fP] − ∂_x/(λ+∂_x) P`.
- David's framing (07:54): horizon-exit noise is Gaussian at low amplitude but with a tail set by "a Euclidean tunnelling instanton"; the KM coefficients would then be needed. Claude: `D^(n≥3) ∼ e^{−S_E}` but, as David noted, `D^(n)ηⁿ/D^(2)η² ∼ e^{−S_E}(η/σ)^{n−2}` **grows in the tail**, so truncation is unsafe (Pawula). Options: characteristic-function/Lévy–Khintchine resummation, MSR with the cumulant generating function `K(∂_φ)`, direct Langevin sampling.
- David (08:43): "The Fokker-Planck equation is the Schrödinger equation for the MSR action" — so approximate the path integral rather than solve the Schrödinger equation. Claude: non-Gaussianity is an obstruction on the FPE side (nonlocal `L`) but "essentially irrelevant on the path integral side — it just changes which trajectory is the instanton."
- **David's two-level structure** (09:06), the thread's key formulation: "the MSR action does not itself compute the fluctuations at horizon exit. Those have to be determined by solving the Mukhanov–Sasaki equation … at low amplitude, and by a Euclidean instanton at large amplitude. The MSR action just stitches together multiple copies of these events." Claude labelled these **Level 1** (single horizon-exit kick, `p(η)`; a UV-theory calculation) and **Level 2** (MSR instanton = optimal sequence of kicks, `S_MSR = Σ −ln p(η_i)`), and noted that if `S_E[η]` grows slower than `η²` the Level-2 saddle may become a *single* Level-1 kick dressed by typical evolution.
